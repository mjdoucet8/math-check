# Numora — planning decisions, 29 September 2026

## Scope and experience
Numora is the working name; the GitHub repository remains `mjdoucet8/math-check`.
Plan the MVP before choosing implementation technology or writing game code.
The initial theme targets Grade 5 students, with adaptive mathematics spanning K–5.
Balance world building with adventure, leaning toward mysterious exploration.
Keep the story simple for students with academic difficulties: short dialogue, one visible goal at a time, optional narration, and clear results.

## World and player
An island's remarkable systems have fallen asleep, while nature and a small resident community remain alive. The world combines ancient technology and magic.
The customizable player character is a newcomer who arrives intentionally.
The first awakening is small and intriguing.
Math appears through restoring mechanisms, helping residents with practical tasks, and discovering journal clues/maps; occasional magical stones or guardians may also be used.
Residents offer reciprocal practical help rather than withholding directions behind unrelated quizzes.
The journal automatically collects discovery pictures; remembering lore must not gate progress.
Exact arrival motivation and energy lore remain open.

## Opening sequence
A resident points to a dormant harbour light: “Can you help me fix this?”
The student completes a short glowing-stone quantity task.
The harbour light turns on, then a matching light flickers across the water.
The resident says, “Look! That one woke up too.”
A nearby path becomes accessible, with a clear marker toward the distant light.
The first reward is a visible world change and a brief musical cue. Introduce a customization item at the next discovery.

## Answer methods
Choose response methods per item according to the mathematical evidence required.
For “Place 5 stones in the tray,” the student must move/select five visible objects. Saying “five” does not show construction of a set.
Remove Speak answer from this interaction; Listen remains available for narrated instructions.
Voice can be used where a spoken response supplies the intended evidence. It is not a universal alternative to manipulation.
Five is an illustrative quantity, not a locked starting difficulty or validated assessment item.

## Mistakes and support
After an incorrect submitted response, the light stays off and stones remain where placed. The student can add/remove stones and retry without restarting.
Use neutral feedback: “You can change the stones, then try again.”
After two unsuccessful submitted attempts, the resident offers optional help: “Want some help?”
Demonstrate counting three separate practice stones, highlighting each once while saying “one, two, three.” Then return to the student's tray to try making five.
If difficulty continues, offer “Let's do it together.” Highlight and count aloud each stone the student places; when five are placed, the light turns on and the adventure continues.
Keep independent responses, assisted attempts, and guided completion distinct. Guided completion is not independent mastery. Revisit the skill independently in a later challenge.
Exact post-demonstration trigger timing and handling extra/removing stones during guided completion remain open.
